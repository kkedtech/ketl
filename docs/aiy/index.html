Agent = Model + Harness. Most people your age can talk to an AI. Very few can build the system around it — the part that actually ships in a product, a portfolio, or a job interview.

Who it's for

Whichever door you're coming through.

New to building with AI

You've used it. You haven't built with it.

Everyone your age has a ChatGPT habit. Almost nobody your age can explain what a harness is, let alone build one. That gap is exactly what employers are starting to screen for — and it's closing fast for the people who move now.

Already technical / at university

This is the layer above your degree.

Most CS and IT courses teach you to write code. Almost none teach you to architect systems around a model — memory, tools, permissions, observability. That's the part that's actually new, and it's not yet taught anywhere formally. Get there first.

Questions we get asked.

+Will this actually help with job applications or university applications?

This builds three concrete, explainable artifacts — a research tool, a persistent AI assistant, and a working agentic system. Each one is a real thing you built and can walk an interviewer or admissions reader through, not a certificate that says you attended something.

+I already know how to code — is this too basic?

No. Explorer is fast if you're already technical. Architect is where this gets genuinely advanced — most working developers haven't built a permissioned tool-calling system from scratch yet. That's the part that's actually rare.

+Is this a bootcamp or a course I do at my own pace?

Live, cohort-based, mentor-led — not self-paced video. You're building alongside people at your level, with someone who's built these systems professionally in the room.

+What's the actual outcome — a certificate?

Three built artifacts you can put in a portfolio or talk through in an interview. That's worth more than a certificate, and it's the actual point.

Build the thing that gets you noticed, not just another line that says "AI-literate."
