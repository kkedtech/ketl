About us

About KETL

Technology is already in every child's hands. The question that matters is whether they're using it - or building it.

KETL – pronounced 'kettle' – stands for Kanga EdTech Vision Labs, and it exists to answer that question with structure, not slogans. We design STEAM → Tech Developer pathways for schools and communities - grounded in Montessori and other Human-Centric Pedagogies and Philosophies and Systems Thinking, built to move learners from consuming technology to creating it.

What we believe:

  • Real education builds independence, not dependency. A child who can follow a tutorial has learned a trick. A child who understands how the system works has learned something that stays.
  • Strong ecosystems - human or technical - emerge from strong foundations. We don't chase surface-level engagement. We build pathways that hold up over years, not terms.
  • Technology should be understood and created, not just consumed. That's the line between a coding class and a developmental ecosystem, and it's the line everything we build sits on.

What we do:

We partner with progressive schools and youth communities to run programs that move children and young people from users to makers - through coding, electronics, robotics, AI and hands-on systems thinking. Coding Club is the hands of this work: the community-facing delivery arm where the pathway becomes real, cohort by cohort.

The longer goal is a self-sustaining model - one that runs on structure, not on any single person in the room - replicable across Australia and beyond.

Our EdTech Founder

Karthik Bharadwaj

Karthik spent 22+ years in global IT - research and development, infrastructure, training, and mentoring - learning how systems scale, and where they quietly fail. That work eventually pulled him toward a different kind of architecture: building learning environments for people, not just machines.

He believes technology should be understood and created, not just consumed. Through KETL and Coding Clubs, he designed and successfully runs STEAM → Developer/Creator pathways inside progressive schools and youth communities - programs that move children and young people from users to makers through coding, electronics, robotics, AI and hands-on systems thinking. His approach draws on Montessori and several other educational traditions, all circling one question: what conditions help young people grow into independent, capable, and creative contributors?

The longer goal is a self-sustaining community model - one that can be replicated across Australia and beyond.

Outside the work, he plays the Indian classical bamboo flute, and tinkers with astronomy, photography, puzzles, and electronic circuits - the same curiosity he tries to spark in the learners he works with. Connect with Karthik on LinkedIn.

The method

Most technology education produces users. That's not a shortage of access — it's a design flaw. A child who only consumes what a screen gives them never learns what's on the other side of it. KETL exists to put children on the other side — building, breaking, rebuilding, on their own terms.

Unplugged before code

Cards, grids and stories first. A child who understands the idea writes better code than one who only knows the syntax.

Small on purpose

Cohorts are capped so every child shows what they made — not what they were told to make.

Failure treated as data

A build that breaks gets rebuilt. That is the lesson, not the setback.

10+ years of practice

Programs successfully running in multiple progressive, community-centric independent schools in Sydney.

For parents

Questions we get asked.

+Does my child need any coding experience?

No. The Unplugged AI session assumes none — it runs mostly screen-free with cards, grids and stories, and ends with a first live block-coding touch.

+How much of this is screen time?

The entry session is mostly screen-free by design. Later stages move onto a screen deliberately, once the child already understands what the code is doing.

+How large are the groups?

Small on purpose. The Holiday AI Sprint is capped at six to nine students per session, and the single session runs as a small group.

+Is it online or in person?

The Holiday AI Sprint runs live online. The weekly Coding Club runs in person or online depending on your location.

+Which one should we start with?

The free parent webinar if you want the whole picture first. The Unplugged AI single session if your child is ready to sit in.

+Can we pay per session?

The weekly Coding Club is priced per session or per term. The Holiday AI Sprint is priced as one 5 or 10 session block.

Whether you're placing one child or building a program for an entire school, the first step is the same.
